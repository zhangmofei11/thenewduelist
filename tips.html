Tips and Tricks for The New Duelist

Small decisions win duels. Use these habits to squeeze more out of every card you own.

Opening Hand Priority

Your first hand decides the tempo of the whole match. Rank every card in it from 1 to 3 before you play turn 1.

  • • Keep one cheap body every game. A 1-2 drop such as Flame Sprite gives you something to do on turn 1, forces the opponent to answer, and keeps you from being tempo-checked for free.
  • • Never keep two expensive cards with no blocker. Holding Inferno Dragon and Earthshaker but no defense means every early board is unwinnable.
  • • One Spell is worth two bodies early. Cards like Frost Elemental or Arcane Burst change a turn's outcome, so a utility card in the opener is usually stronger than a second attacker.
  • • Mutate your keeper for the matchup. Against a fast Wind board, hold Stone Golem over a second body. Against a slow Earth Ramp shell, hold a second body over a wall.

Resource Chaining Order

Mana is the real clock in The New Duelist, and cards played out of order are how players run out of steam. Chain your resources in the order below instead of dumping your biggest card whenever it becomes playable.

Turn Priority Play Why It Comes First
Turn 1 Cheapest body or a mana accelerator Establishes board presence and card advantage before the opponent sets up
Turn 2 Second body, or the answer to their turn 1 Answers are worth more than bodies when the board is contested
Turn 3 Utility: spell, heal, or board clear Utility swings the widest range of outcomes and is the hardest to rebuild
Turn 4-5 Your first big threat Finishing pressure before the opponent stabilises, while you still have answers left
Turn 6+ Finisher plus one held answer An empty hand at turn 6 is a guaranteed loss to any late-game threat

Matchup-Specific Openers

Against Aggro (Fire)

They want to hit 8000 damage by turn 6, so you have to make that timeline expensive.

  • • Open with a defense card, not a second attacker
  • • Save one board clear for their biggest swing turn
  • • Trade, do not race: every Flame Sprite they spend is a card they no longer have

Against Control (Water)

They want to outlast you, which means you cannot let the game reach their preferred shape.

  • • Force the game forward with early threats
  • • Do not over-commit into a healthy Tidal Guardian
  • • Keep one big finisher back for after their sustain is spent

Against Ramp (Earth)

They plan to cheat out expensive cards, so pressure and disruption beat raw stats.

  • • Deny their mana growth instead of matching it
  • • Apply pressure in the turns before they hit their big turn
  • • Beat their plan, not their board, since the board arrives late

Against Wind Tempo

They chain attacks for multiplier damage and rely on speed, so speed denial is the key.

  • • Slow them with defensive cards and stun effects
  • • Take the first trade: removing one link breaks the chain
  • • Keep a heal ready, since burst damage lands before you can react

Reading Your Opponent

Card Advantage Tells

  • • Early turn 1 pass: a hand of only expensive cards, usually a ramp shell
  • • They keep playing cards after a heavy trade: they have more answers than you assumed
  • • Fast Wind turn 1: expect a tempo deck, not a control deck

Cooldown and Commitment Tells

  • • A heavy card at turn 3: their finisher is already in hand and they are ahead of schedule
  • • No cards played for a full turn: they are holding a burst, usually an Inferno Dragon swing
  • • Repeated small blocks: they are protecting a single large threat, so look for it

Mistake Analysis

Mistake Why It Loses Games Fix
Playing your biggest card turn 2 It leaves you with no answer and no card advantage Hold it for turn 4-5 and keep a body in reserve
Over-blocking a small threat You spend two cards to kill one and fall behind on cards Let cheap bodies trade, block the 2400+ threats
Playing around one element only Arcane opponents like the Arcane Sentinel switch elements each turn Keep one off-element answer in every deck
Chasing a bad trade You are three cards down to win one race you did not need Compare board totals, not individual card values

Practice Drills

Ranked games are expensive ways to learn. Run these short drills in practice mode instead, and track the result in a note on your phone.

Ten-Hand Drill

Open ten practice games and only play cards in the chain order above. Do not win the game. Win the order.

One-Card Hand Drill

Play ten games holding exactly one card in hand at all times. If you cannot win, you win because you never empty your hand.

Guess-the-Element Drill

Before each match, write down the opponent's element. Review after: three correct guesses in a row means you are reading boards, not just hands.
