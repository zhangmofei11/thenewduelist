The New Duelist Beginner Guide

From your first battle in Hearth to ranked play: what to learn, in what order, and when to move on.

Your First Session

Start With One Element

You can pick Fire, Water, Earth or Wind starter decks. Commit to one for your first ten battles. The bonus from focused elements makes the early game far easier to read, and you will stop making the classic beginner mistake of spreading across five elements with no plan.

  • • Fire: fastest damage, weakest defence, easiest to learn first
  • • Water: slowest start, but the most forgiving once you survive
  • • Earth: tanky and steady, teaches resource planning
  • • Wind: aggressive and fast, teaches sequencing

Learn the Four Systems

  • • Mana. It grows each turn and you can only spend what you have. Unspent mana is wasted value.
  • • Card types. Attack cards deal damage, Defense cards absorb it, Spell cards change the board, Balanced cards do a bit of everything.
  • • ATK and DEF. A card with 2500 ATK and 1800 DEF (Inferno Dragon) is not balanced on both sides. Read the numbers before you commit.
  • • HP and cooldowns. Bosses and some cards have cooldowns between uses, which is what makes baiting them possible.

Campaign Progression Milestones

Milestone 1

Finish Chapter 1: The Spark of Battle

Three battles against local rivals in Hearth, ending with the Ember Scout Captain at 1500 HP. Do not rush this. It is the cheapest place to learn the turn structure, and the Ember Peaks reward you with the Fire affinity and your first real deck options.

Milestone 2

Clear Chapter 2: Trial by Fire

Five battles and the Ember Arena tournament, ending with Pyromancer Vex at 3000 HP. Vex applies burn damage over time, so this is the first boss that punishes greed. Bring healing, keep your HP above half, and unlock the Arena if you have not already.

Milestone 3

Reach the Azure Depths

Chapters 3 and 4 shift the rules of the fight: water puzzles and currents change card play, and the Leviathan Knight at 4000 HP forces you to chip down a high-defence target rather than race it. Queen Tidalis at 5000 HP then punishes you for leaving summons alive.

Milestone 4

Master the Stone Giants and Sky Citadel

The Granite Guardian (6000 HP) and Elder Titan Moloch (7000 HP) teach patience and phase timing, since Moloch doubles attack at half HP. Storm Warden Zephyr and Cloud King Altius in the Sky Citadel teach speed and summon clearing, because Altius heals from his own clones. By Chapter 8 you should be able to build a deck on purpose rather than by accident.

Milestone 5

Arcane Core and Ranked

The Arcane Sentinel (6500 HP) switches elements every turn, so element-locked decks finally break. Only after that should you enter ranked leagues. A balanced deck with answers for Fire, Water and Arcane is what carries you through the three-phase Arcane Council finale, and it is the same deck that wins your first ranked matches.

Building Your First Real Deck

Do not collect 500 cards and try to use them all. A deck of 20-24 cards is the working size, and the split matters more than the individual cards you pick.

Slot How Many What It Does
Attack 8-10 Your win condition. Inferno Dragon and Storm Phantom push damage, Earthshaker gives burst
Defense 6-8 Buys you turns. Tidal Guardian sustains, Stone Golem holds a lane
Spell 4-6 Utility and answers. Frost Elemental slows a threat, Arcane Burst punishes a wide board
Balanced 1-2 Arcane Sovereign slots into any plan and smooths awkward early turns

Beginner Mistakes to Avoid

Decking Too Wide

A 30-card deck with five elements draws the wrong half of your deck every game. Cut to 20-24, pick one main element and one support element, and add the off-element answer later once you know the matchup.

Skipping the Practice Mode Check

Run your deck in practice mode before you queue ranked. It costs you nothing and it is where you discover that you have no answer to a Storm Phantom board, or that your only cheap card is a dead draw late.

Ignoring the Farming Loop

Daily quests, replaying Chapter 2 for gold, Arena card packs and trading duplicate cards are how a beginner actually acquires a competitive pool. Push content, not just wins, in your first weeks.

Playing Every Card Every Turn

Emptying your hand is how beginners lose. Keep one card in reserve so you can respond to the board in front of you rather than only to the plan you made on turn 1.

Your First Week Checklist

  • Pick one starter element and stay in it for ten battles
  • Learn the turn structure and mana curve in the first three chapters
  • Beat Pyromancer Vex with healing in your deck
  • Open the Arena and run one practice match
  • Claim every daily quest reward
  • Trim your deck to 20-24 cards with 6-8 defensive cards
  • Add one off-element answer
  • Clear the Stone Giants without losing more than three attempts
  • Trade your duplicate cards
  • Enter one ranked match with a deck you have tested twice
